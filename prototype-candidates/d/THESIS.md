# Candidate D · Plant View

**Thesis: the metaphor is the personalization.** Rivals styled the pipeline; D lets the customer's industry style it.
For Acme Manufacturing the canvas renders as their own plant floor: documents are raw stock in a silo, ai_parse_document is a scanning vessel, the Agent Bricks labeler is a routing valve with an instrument bubble, lanes are tanks on a manifold, UC lineage is lot traceability (BATCH TRACE), Caught vs Cost is a yield meter, and Supervisor is the shift supervisor on the plant intercom.

**Why it wins:** it is the only candidate whose aesthetic is itself the product claim. DocFlow's headline feature is the per-customer demo designer ("their demo, their vocabulary"), and this design proves that claim on sight; switch the profile and the plant re-skins (Meridian Health would render as a care pathway). Manufacturing execs read schematics and control rooms natively, control-room visual language is literally engineered for wall projection, and every element maps one-to-one to a real platform capability with honest GA/BETA/PREVIEW plates, so nothing on screen is decoration.

**What it sacrifices:** industry neutrality (a generic back-office profile needs a second archetype, so the canvas costs more to build per industry than one universal conveyor); some per-screen density versus the baseline; and whimsy, since the industrial register runs serious. The bet: a boardroom remembers "they drew our plant" longer than any board of tiles.
